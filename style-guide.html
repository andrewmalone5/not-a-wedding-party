The brief

One identity, two registers.

The save-the-date set a clear direction: a saturated party red field with grainy paper texture, warm cream typography, and an etched cocktail illustration. The visual mood is cocktail-party energy without the formality — a confident modern serif paired with a soft hand-script, anchored by a single bold colour.

This guide extends that moment into a system the website can run on. Two registers carry it: a celebratory register (red surfaces, heavy display type, script accents) for hero, divider and footer moments — and a functional register (cream surfaces, dark warm ink, sans-serif body) for the long-read content guests actually rely on.

Default to the functional register. Use the celebratory register for moments that deserve it.

Palette

One red. One cream. The rest earns its place.

The system is built on two near-equal partners — party red and poster cream — supported by a warm dark ink and a small set of derivative tones for shadows, accents, and overlays.

Party Red #D63D3F · --red Primary brand. Hero, footer, accents, primary buttons.
Red Deep #A82B2D · --red-deep Hover, pressed, link underlines on cream.
Burgundy #6B1E20 · --burgundy Deep accents. Dark text on cream when red feels too loud.
Blush #E89C92 · --blush Tinted illustration overlay. Use sparingly.
Poster Cream #F2E7D2 · --cream Primary on-red text. Card fills on cream.
Cream Soft #FAF1DD · --cream-soft Page background for body sections.
Ink #1A0F10 · --ink Headings, primary body copy on cream.
Ink Soft #5C3A3B · --ink-soft Secondary copy, captions on cream.

Rules of thumb

  • Don't tint the red. Use it at full strength. If something feels too loud, change the layout, not the saturation.
  • Cream beats white. Pure white cuts the warmth of the system; reach for cream-soft instead.
  • Long-form copy lives on cream — never set more than 4–5 lines of body copy on red.
  • Two colours, one accent. Treat anything beyond red + cream + ink as a once-per-page accent.

Typography

A bold serif, a hand-script, and a quiet sans.

Three families, each with one job. The display serif carries the moment, the script carries the warmth, and the sans carries everything you actually read.

Voice

Warm, direct, a little Belfast.

Write the way you'd talk to a friend who's helping you plan. Confident, not formal. Specific, not corporate. A wee bit Northern Irish.

  • Use contractions — "you'll", "we're", "don't". Formality kills the vibe.
  • Drop in local idiom sparingly — "you'll be grand", "the craic", "wee" — once or twice per section, not every line.
  • Lean into specificity — "£10–£15 from Belfast City Airport" beats "affordable taxis."
  • Em-dashes, not semicolons — they fit the conversational tone.
  • Avoid wedding-industrial language — no "nuptials", no "celebrating our union". This is a party, not a ceremony.

Texture

A wee bit of grain.

The save-the-date has a soft paper-grain texture across the red field. We replicate this with a procedural SVG noise filter applied at low opacity (~18%) over red surfaces. It adds warmth without weight — no images to load.

  • Apply via the .grain class. It uses a ::after overlay with mix-blend-mode: multiply.
  • Only on red surfaces. On cream the grain reads as dirt rather than texture.
  • Don't stack. One grain layer per section. Never on top of dense type — keep it on backgrounds and large fields.

Illustration

Vintage etched, tinted, sparing.

The poster's cocktail glass establishes the language: detailed engraved/etched line illustrations, often vintage botanical or barware references, tinted into the red field at reduced opacity so they read as a wash rather than a foreground subject.

Do

  • Pick illustrations with strong line-work and visible cross-hatching.
  • Convert to a single colour (cream on red, or red on cream) — never full colour.
  • Use at 25–60% opacity so they sit behind type, not in front of it.
  • Theme around cocktail bar / Belfast / Irish countryside — glasses, citrus, botanicals, bridges, dock cranes.

Don't

  • Use modern flat-vector or 3D illustration. It will fight the system.
  • Mix illustration styles within a page.
  • Place illustrations behind body copy at high opacity — readability beats decoration.
  • Use generic stock wedding imagery (rings, doves, hearts in colour).

Phase 2

Illustration is currently a stretch goal. The site ships without it — typography, colour, and grain carry the identity until we have illustration assets we love. Public-domain etchings (Internet Archive, Old Book Illustrations) are a good starting point.
