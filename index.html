The Party

Titanic Distillers, Thompson Dock

One of Belfast's most extraordinary spaces. Titanic Distillers at Thompson Dock sits inside the historic Pumphouse — the very building that once drained the dry dock where the Titanic herself was fitted out before her maiden voyage in 1912. It's been beautifully restored and is now Belfast's first working whiskey distillery in almost 90 years.

The dock outside is 850 feet long and was purpose-built to hold the largest ships ever constructed. Standing in it, you feel the full scale of what was once built here. The air is thick with history and great Irish whiskey.

Getting here

Flying in? Here's what we'd suggest.

If you're coming from abroad, Dublin Airport is often the better-connected option, with more international routes. We suggest arriving 3 or 4 September, with your departure from 6 September onwards. Fly with Ryanair or Aer Lingus.

Dublin Airport to Belfast

The journey is roughly two hours depending on your route.

Bus Recommended

The easiest and cheapest option. Aircoach runs a direct service from Dublin Airport to Belfast city centre with drop-offs on Glengall Street near the Europa Bus Centre.

Coaches run regularly throughout the day and into the evening. Tickets are around €12–€20 each way; about 2 hours.

No car, no parking, no stress. Best if you're travelling solo or as a couple.

Book on aircoach.ie ↗

Hire car

Best for groups, or if you fancy exploring Ireland beyond Belfast — works out cheaper per person. About 90 minutes by car.

One important note: you're crossing an international border from the Republic into Northern Ireland, so confirm with your hire car company that cross-border driving is included in your policy.

Train

There's no direct train from Dublin Airport. You'd take a bus or taxi into Dublin city centre first, then catch the Enterprise service to Belfast Lanyon Place.

Scenic and comfortable, but adds time and a connection. Better suited if you're already staying in Dublin.

A note on the border

There's no passport control between the Republic of Ireland and Northern Ireland — you won't even notice you've crossed until your phone signal changes and the markings on the road look slightly different.

The main thing to be aware of is the currency change from Euros to GBP. Fill up on fuel on the Dublin side if you're driving — prices can differ.

Getting around

Belfast is compact and walkable.

The Titanic Quarter is about a 20–25 minute walk from the city centre, or a short taxi ride.

The venue: Titanic Distillers at Thompson Dock, Queen's Road, Belfast BT3 9DT.

Show your driver the address above or simply say "Thompson Dock, Titanic Quarter" — any Belfast driver will know exactly where you mean.

  • Walking — the best way to see the city. Flat, well-paved, and the distances are very manageable.
  • Taxis — cheap, plentiful, card-friendly. Value Cabs and fonaCab are the local go-tos, both with apps. Perfect for the journey home at 2am.
  • Uber — works in Belfast. Reliable for late nights and airport runs. Expect £5–£12 for most city-centre journeys.
  • The Glider — Belfast's rapid bus service, runs every 4–9 minutes across the city.
  • Belfast Bikes — 400+ bikes, 60 docking stations, £1 per 30 minutes. Great for daytime exploring.
  • From the airport — taxis run £10–£15 from Belfast City Airport, or £25–£35 from Belfast International.

Belfast tips

Worth a visit.

Belfast has transformed into one of the most exciting cities in Europe. Here's how to make the most of your weekend.

Titanic Quarter

Where the party is. Visit Titanic Belfast museum, walk the Maritime Mile, and look up at Samson and Goliath, the iconic Harland & Wolff cranes that still dominate the skyline.

Cathedral Quarter

The city's best bar scene, cobbled streets, and colourful street art. The Duke of York, Dirty Onion, and The Spaniard are essential stops.

City Centre

City Hall, Victoria Square for shopping, and the Crown Liquor Saloon — a Victorian gin palace and National Trust property. Worth a pint of Guinness.

St George's Market

Friday or Saturday morning, don't miss it. Fresh food, live music, some of the best street food in Ireland. (Order a belly-buster soda bread if you want grub.)

Insider tip

Book a Black Cab political mural tour. One of the most powerful things you can do in Belfast — book in advance on weekends. A real insight into Belfast taxi-men culture, the history, and stories from the Troubles.

What to pack

Belfast will reward you for being prepared. A little forward planning means you can focus entirely on having the best weekend.

Clothing — dress for Irish weather, not Irish summer

The golden rule in Ireland: layers. September can give you all four seasons in one day — mild sunshine in the afternoon, a sharp breeze by evening, and a shower you didn't see coming.

  • A waterproof or water-resistant outer layer.
  • Comfortable walking shoes — Belfast's streets are cobbled in places.
  • Smart shoes or heels for the party.
  • A few layers you can mix and match across the weekend.

From mainland Europe — a quick checklist

  • UK travel adaptor (Type G, three-pin)
  • Valid passport or ID — needed to fly, and some venues ask for ID on the door
  • Travel insurance — always worth having
  • Your card set up for GBP transactions
  • Download Uber, Value Cabs or fonaCab apps before you arrive

UK plug is a three-pin Type G — your European two-pin plugs won't fit without an adaptor. Pick one up before you travel, or grab one at Dublin Airport on the way through.

Good to know

Currency

Belfast is in Northern Ireland — the currency is British Pounds Sterling (£ / GBP). Not Euros.

  • Card is king. Contactless accepted virtually everywhere.
  • Apple Pay and Google Pay both work seamlessly.
  • ATMs widely available in the city centre if you want cash.
  • Belfast is generally cheaper than London, Dublin, or Berlin — pints, food, and taxis are all very reasonable.
